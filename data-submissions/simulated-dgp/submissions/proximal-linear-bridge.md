# Proximal inference with a linear confounding bridge: workflow pilot

## Research question

- **Question and intended use.** Can proximal methods recover a treatment effect and calibrated uncertainty when an unobserved common cause affects treatment and outcome, but measured proxy variables provide information about it? This is a literature-based simulation proposal for testing the submission and review workflow. The mechanism is already represented in the earlier internal collection; it is not claimed as a new DGP. Please treat it as a documentation/workflow pilot and check duplication before any catalog or evaluation decision.
- **Behavior under test.** The case isolates hidden confounding with a correctly specified linear outcome bridge and a constant treatment effect. It checks effect recovery and interval calibration under proxy-based identification. It does not test heterogeneous-effect recovery, weak-proxy limits or a calibrated clinical population. Official evaluation inclusion would be a separate curator decision.

## Study design and data source

- **Motivation and references.** The mechanism follows Cui, Pu, Shi, Miao and Tchetgen Tchetgen, *Semiparametric Proximal Causal Inference*, JASA 119(546), 1348-1359 (2024), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2023.2191817). The [open manuscript v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.08411v4), Section 5.1, printed pp. 21-22, equation (13), and Appendix H specify the Gaussian proxy construction and compatible bridge. The pilot retains that fixed literature scenario. The sample size, repeated-table pilot, shared-fold estimator comparison and workflow packaging are project choices, rather than results reported by the source paper.
- **Population and sampling.** Each row is an independent synthetic individual; there are no clusters, repeated measurements or time-series dependencies. The proposed comparison attempts 100 independent tables of n=500 under one fixed scenario and one prespecified baseline query shared by methods. Every table is used for both methods; there is no scenario mixture or scenario weighting. The private implementation checks have generated 100 such tables, but no new PFN/RF performance results are claimed by this proposal.

## DAG and identification

- **Variables and causal structure.** Observed variables are baseline covariates X=(X1,X2), binary treatment A, treatment-side proxy Z, outcome-side proxy W and continuous outcome Y. U is unobserved. A compatible causal account allows X and U to cause the proxies, treatment and outcome, Z to predict treatment, W to affect outcome, and A to affect outcome, with no direct Z-to-Y pathway. W is baseline with respect to treatment, not a treatment-induced mediator. The paper describes retrospective draws using p(X)p(A|X)p(Z,W,U|A,X); that computational order is not a causal DAG and does not assert A causes U or W. Compatibility of the causal interpretation is part of the scientific review.
- **Assumptions.** The target uses consistency, no interference, treatment positivity and exchangeability given (U,X), together with the proxy restrictions Y independent of Z given (A,U,X) and W independent of (A,Z) given (U,X). The private specification checks Gaussian covariance and mean-shift restrictions that preserve these properties and gives an outcome bridge satisfying the conditional moment equation. The required bridge/identification and completeness conditions are grounded in the source construction and require reviewer assessment; finite simulation tests do not prove them. No assumption violation is deliberately introduced in this fixed scenario.

## Data-generating process and estimand

- **Observed input.** A table has six observed columns X1, X2, A, Z, W and Y, with one row per individual. The estimator also receives the same prespecified two-dimensional query x*. The numerical adapter uses X of shape (n,2), Z and W of shape (n,1), and A and Y of shape (n,). U, potential outcomes, oracle conditional means and scoring references are not estimator inputs. There are no missing values or categorical covariates in this pilot.
- **Generator and scenarios.** Baseline covariates are Gaussian, A conditional on X is Bernoulli with a logistic probability, and the proxy/latent vector conditional on (A,X) is Gaussian subject to the source constraints. Y combines treatment, covariates, an oracle conditional proxy mean, residual proxy variation and independent Gaussian noise. The private generator records the precise draw order, covariance, coefficients and seed schedule. Only the original linear, constant-effect scenario is proposed; parameters stay fixed across replicate tables. Fresh pilot draws are separate from all existing official frozen test tables.
- **Estimand.** The primary target is the population CATE at the common query, $\tau(x^*)=\mathbb{E}[Y(1)-Y(0)\mid X=x^*]$, in Y units for the intervention contrast A=1 versus A=0. In this constant-effect scenario it also equals the population ATE. It is not a sample-specific treatment contrast or ATT.
- **Truth check.** The compatible outcome bridge has a constant treatment contrast. Integrating $h(W,1,x^*)-h(W,0,x^*)$ over natural W conditional on X=x* yields the analytic reference; drawing W from its observational distribution conditional on assigned A would change the target and must not be substituted. The private derivation also verifies the same constant using potential outcomes. The reference has no Monte Carlo approximation error. The local receipt checks the algebraic contrast and empirical bridge moments; the exact scoring value and parameter files remain in private review.

## Benchmark comparison

- **Output and comparator.** The planned comparator is ProximalFM versus an RF-assisted proximal bridge/GMM baseline, receiving identical observed tables and query points. Both return a CATE point estimate and a nominal 95% interval. RF uses 200 trees, min_samples_leaf=5, single-thread execution, shared fixed five-fold labels for first-stage conditional moments, and B=50 entire-table IID full-refit bootstrap with fresh folds per resample and a percentile interval. The final bridge and query-point moment fits use the full current table. ProximalFM uses the same initial partition through five 400-row contexts and an equal mixture of native predictive distributions; this is fold-context ensembling, not the same nuisance-model cross-fitting interface. No model is given U or an oracle bridge.
- **Scoring and replication.** Across the 100 attempted paired tables report signed bias, sample error variance (ddof=1), RMSE, inclusive empirical coverage, mean interval length and mean per-table inference time. Report raw Y units and per-table observed-sd(Y) normalized error/length summaries; coverage and time are not scaled. Primary paired summaries use the common successful set with finite points and valid intervals, alongside all attempted, failed and missing counts and exclusions. Do not replace failed tables or silently drop failed bootstrap draws. RF time includes its point estimate, all 50 refits and interval calculation; PFN time includes the five-context native inference and aggregation. Exclude loading, explicit warmup and file I/O for both, and report the CPU/software environment. This describes a proposed comparison, not newly completed scores; the local generator validation is separate.
